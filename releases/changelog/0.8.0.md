## [0.8.0] - 2026-10-20

### Added

- **Forces now travel to the destination you give them.**
  Select the arrow icon, click an active Force — or one member of it, to
  split that member and its followers off — then click a destination.
  The Force steers itself tile by tile around woods, hills and rivers
  at its Leader's original pace for the ground under it, rests when the
  Leader tires, and sets off again in the morning (#10).
- **Characters can put down and take objects, and the dead leave what they carried.** Objects lying on the map now live in the simulation's 30 placement slots from the start of the game: putting one down fills the first free slot, taking one frees it, taking the Ring makes the taker the Ring-bearer, and a Character killed in a fight drops everything it carried where it fell. Saved games keep objects carried and placed (#104).
- **The provisions sack on the Animation Level now puts down, takes and uses objects.** Its windows list what the chosen Character carries or what lies where the Force stands, in the original's wording, and the blue potion, the black flask, the glowing vial and the coil of rope work as they did (#105).
- **Tokens mobilise their Nations, and the Palantír shows its vision.** Using an object in the simulation now has its effect: the Dwarven Ring, the Red Arrow, the sceptre and the silver orb mobilise Dáin, Théoden, Denethor and Thranduil when shown to them, and give a hint anywhere else. The Palantír may show the vision of the nine and set the Dark Lord's war going early, or else give an advisor's tip. The blue potion, black flask and glowing vial heal and strengthen. The view's Use list follows in #105 (#106).
- **The end of the game shows the original's closing scenes.** Behind the closing pages, a lost war shows Mount Doom with the Dark Lord's host, and a won war shows the Fellowship gathered at Hobbiton. The scene shows alone for a few seconds, then each page appears in a message box over it (#107).
- **The Ring passes to a new bearer, and the Ring can win or lose the game.** When the Ring-bearer falls in a fight, the winners take the Ring: a Hobbit if one is left, otherwise the last of the winners still standing, with "<name> now has the RING.". An Evil bearer sets off for Barad-dûr and the other Nazgûl scatter. The Ring-bearer's Force standing on Mount Doom at the hour wins the game, and an Evil Leader carrying the Ring onto Barad-dûr loses it, each with the original's closing pages (#107).
- **The game has its music.** The original's own scores play through a port of its Sonix music driver: the title music at the start, a piece for each Place with scenery of its own on the Animation Level, themes for the Characters met, music for fights and for the end of the game, and a bell as each message box opens. Q switches sound off and on (#132).
- **Fights on the Animation Level have their sound effects.** A figure's swing plays a whoosh or a sword, and a spell plays magic, as in the original (#133).
- **The Full Map Level looks like the original without a Kickstart ROM.** When `bun run export:web` finds no ROM for topaz/8, Galadriel's Mirror and the Archive window are lettered in the port's own hand-drawn 8 by 8 font in its spirit, rather than lord/10 at double width (#135).
- **Spells now show a travelling flash and a burst on the Animation Level.**
  Wizards, hooded figures and Balrogs use the original figure scripts and
  colours for the effect (#141).
- **The Full Map Level's original art and icons are ready for the web view.**
  The asset export identifies the 640 by 200 map and its four icons, and includes
  their palette with an explicit provisional label until the original colours
  are recovered (#29).
- **The original game's graphics and text can now be exported for the web build.**
  One command (`bun run export:web`) writes the Campaign Level tile atlas and
  palette, the tile map, every decoded bitmap and the game's text as web-ready
  PNG and JSON files, with a manifest listing each asset (#3).
- **The Full Map Level shows the whole of Middle Earth with a dot for every Force, in the original's colours.**
  Evil Forces are red, Neutral ones green, and the player's blink. The map is
  drawn hi-res at 640 by 200 in the original palette, and time stands still
  while it is shown. Until moving between Levels arrives, open it with
  `?level=full-map` (#30).
- **The player moves up to the Full Map Level and back down to the Campaign Level.**
  The map icon or `U` goes up; the magnifying glass or `M` turns the pointer
  into a magnifying glass, and clicking the map opens the Campaign Level
  centred on that spot. Time stops on the Full Map Level and resumes at the
  same Game Speed on the way down (#31).
- **Galadriel's Mirror shows every Force on the move.**
  The Full Map Level's eye, or `S`, lists the moving Good Forces with where
  they are going or whom they follow, and the moving Evil Forces by name
  only, as the original does. A press anywhere closes it (#32).
- **The game can be saved and restored from the Full Map Level.**
  The scroll, or `A`, opens the original's archive window: save the current
  game to the single slot in the browser, or restore it. A restore stays on
  the Full Map Level, and a save from another version is refused (#33).
- **The Game Speed can be changed from the Full Map Level.**
  Its hourglass, or `T`, opens the same Game Speed window as on the Campaign
  Level, and the setting carries over between Levels. Time still stands still
  on the Full Map Level (#34).
- **Forces that meet an enemy now fight.** Evil and Good Leaders that come within reach of each other fight it out round by round, by the original's rules: Characters are wounded and killed, Units lose men and are destroyed, and followers of a fallen Leader carry on alone. Game time stands still during a fight. Games saved before this release can't be restored (#46).
- **Choose how to fight.** When a fight starts you are told who is attacked and can "Go there" or "Ignore it". Going there opens the combat window over the map, where you set Charge, Engage, Withdraw or Retreat for each of your Characters and Units as the losses are reported round by round. Ignoring it, or not answering within about 14 seconds, settles the fight at once and tells you the result and who died (#47).
- **Marching on the Isen Ford or the gates of Mordor now changes the war.**
  When an Evil army reaches the Isen Ford, Gondor rises at once and
  Rohan's musters follow the next morning. When a Good Force reaches the
  approaches to Mordor, the Dark Lord's armies march the next day,
  earlier than the calendar would have them (#49).
- **The war can now be lost, and won, on the battlefield.** If the Garrisons of three of the five Strongholds (Minas Tirith, Lórien, Thranduil's Palace, Edoras and the Hornburg) are destroyed, you lose the war. If the great Orc Garrison of Barad-dûr is destroyed, you win. Games saved before this release can't be restored (#50).
- **The war now keeps its own calendar.** At 8 a.m. each day the game
  checks the War Events as the original did: Saruman marches on
  20 February, the Dark Lord on 10 March, and a mobilised Rohan or
  Gondor, a fallen Gandalf or an early start to the war sets the rest.
  Forces waking for them, and their announcements, follow in later
  changes. Saves from earlier versions can no longer be restored (#58).
- **Forces now wake and join the war when the original woke them.**
  Aragorn, Elrond and others rise as the Ring-bearer comes near, Théoden
  when Gandalf reaches him, and the armies of Gondor, Rohan, the Elves,
  the Dwarves, Saruman and Sauron set out when their War Event comes,
  some of them days later. A woken Good Force comes under your command
  and starts along its original route (#59).
- **The great turns of the war are announced.** On 20 February "Saruman's
  army is on the march!" and on 10 March "The Dark Lord's armies march
  forth!" appear at the top of the Campaign Level for a few seconds while
  the war goes on, as in the original. When an early march on Mordor
  brings the war forward, only Saruman's message comes (#60).
- **Friendly Forces now meet as they did in the original.**
  When a moving Leader reaches a Leader that is not Evil, the game stops
  for a meeting if one of them is mobilised: one may follow the other,
  or an advisor such as Elrond invites it to talk. Following makes the
  two one Force, and Gollum slips away from any Force led by a Wizard.
  Until the meeting window arrives, every meeting answers "Continue on"
  at once (#69).
- **Game time now passes on the Campaign Level.**
  The clock starts on 23 September 1418 and advances a quarter-hour at
  a time, with the date shown under the map. The hourglass icon (or T)
  opens the Game Speed window — Normal, Hasty or Very Hasty — and P
  pauses (#7).
- **Meetings now ask you what to do.** When two friendly Forces meet, a
  window at the top of the map says who has met whom, as the original
  words it ("FARAMIR meets EOMER." / "EOMER welcomes FARAMIR to join."),
  and offers to follow, or to go there, or to continue on. The war waits
  for your answer, and after about 14 seconds the window continues on by
  itself (#70).
- **The Campaign Level map now appears in the browser and scrolls.**
  The web build renders the original tile map with its icon column, and
  moving the pointer to the edge of the screen scrolls across the whole
  map, as in the original; you can also drag the map to pan (#8).
- **The Animation Level's original art is ready for the web view.**
  The asset export now writes the Animation Level's 32-colour palette, every
  figure set as a sheet with its frames and hot spots, the icons, the window
  frame and the scene and script tables from the original's executable (#86).
- **The game opens on the Animation Level, as the original does.** Frodo, Sam and Pippin walk across the bridge below Hobbiton, in scenery built from the original's own layers, and once they have walked off the game goes up to the Campaign Level. The map icon or U goes up at any time, and the Campaign Level's magnifying glass or M goes down to the Force under it (#87).
- **Forces now meet the Encounters waiting across Middle-earth.** Orcs, trolls and balrogs attack a passing Force unless it evades them, and are never used up; Rangers, hobbits and other wanderers pass by the Character you watch on the Animation Level; and Círdan, Tom Bombadil, Galadriel, Treebeard and Radagast stop a Force to invite it to talk, each met once, save Radagast, who comes back. The talks and pass-by messages are shown in a later change (#88).
- **Meet advisors and hear their advice on the Animation Level.** Choose
  "Go there" to hear three boxes of advice, with each tip given once per
  session, or continue on. Passing Encounters now show their messages (#89).
- **The starting Forces now stand on the Campaign Level.**
  All 42 Forces awake at the start appear at their original positions,
  Characters as figures and Units as shield badges, with the Forces you
  can command flashing. Pointing at a Force or a Place names it, and
  clicking a Force opens the window listing everyone in it, Leader
  first (#9).
- **Character fights play out on the Animation Level.** Both sides walk to
  battle, charge and fall as the fight unfolds, and surviving Good figures
  walk on afterwards. Combat orders and results use the same rules as the
  combat window (#90).
- **The Council of Elrond is held when the Ring-bearer reaches Rivendell with Gandalf there.** The Fellowship forms under Gandalf, the hobbits and Aragorn are healed, the calendar moves on by up to two months, the Nazgûl go to sleep, and Bilbo can now be met at Rivendell. The Council's message holds the game for 24 seconds, as the original's did.

### Changed

- **Windows look like the original's.** Every window on the Campaign and Full Map Levels is drawn on parchment inside the original's ragged frame, with dark lettering and the original's red and blue. The info window sits where the original's does, and the combat window's buttons line up with the original's (#75).

### Fixed

- **Windows are lettered in the original's font.** Every window now draws its text in lord/10, the bitmap font the game loads from its disk, pixel for pixel in the palette's colours. Boxes are sized and text is wrapped by the font's real glyph widths, so the Palantír's longer vision fits its box rather than spilling below the frame (#117).
- **Windows on the Animation Level are beige parchment again, not patched with yellow.** As in the original, their fill and the line along their plain edges take colours 1 and 27 of that Level's palette (#118).
- **Characters wear their original colours on the Animation Level and winning end pages.** Gandalf and the two men in the winning picture now use their own colour variants (#119).
- **The Campaign Level's date line reads as the original's.** It now shows the time first, as "5 p.m. September 23, 1418 s.r.", in dark lettering on a parchment strip along the bottom of the screen (#123).
- **Galadriel's Mirror is lettered as in the original.** Its heading is blue and set in from the rows, and the Full Map Level's windows use the Amiga's topaz/8 font, taken from a Kickstart 1.3 ROM when `bun run export:web` finds one in `FS_UAE_DIR` (#124).
- **The Provisions menu's buttons are one width, as in the original.** "Put down an object", "Take an object" and "Use an object" now share the first button's width, with frames and clickable areas to match (#125).
- **The Sword sound effect plays.** The original's Disk 2 sword can't play the note a blow asks for, so it never sounded; the port now plays the Sword from Disk 1, so every blow is heard (#133).
- **Ordering part of a Force now splits it off as the original did.**
  A Character given an Order leaves with its followers, takes its
  Leader's facing as well as its position, and forgets its Default
  Orders. A Unit that follows someone can no longer be ordered on its
  own (#71).
- **Game Speeds now run at the original's rates.** A quarter-hour takes
  5.12 seconds on Normal and 0.6 seconds on Hasty; Very Hasty advances
  with each drawn frame. Message boxes with no buttons close after
  1.2 seconds on Very Hasty and 4 seconds otherwise (#82).
- **Very Hasty runs at the same pace on every display.** It advances one
  quarter-hour every 20 ms, the original's 50 Hz frame, instead of once
  per drawn frame, so a 144 Hz screen no longer runs it faster (#99).
